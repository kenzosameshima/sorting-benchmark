    Methodology and known limitations
    • Times include instrumentation. The comparison/swap/movement counters run inside the timed region, so measured times are higher than the pure algorithm, by very different amounts per algorithm and per compiler (insertion and heap sort are the most affected). Prefer the counters for machine-independent comparisons and use times mainly for shapes and trends.
    • Statistics are recomputed from the per-repetition CSV: sample standard deviation, Student-t 95% CI of the mean, and bootstrap 95% CIs (1000 resamples, fixed seed) for ratios of medians. summary.csv reports a population standard deviation and is not used.
    • Paired ratios. Every algorithm sorts a copy of the same dataset in each repetition, so ratios against the baseline are computed per repetition.
    • Counters are deterministic. With the same seed they must be identical on every OS and compiler; any difference is a bug, not noise.
    • Cross-OS time ratios also mix compiler and libc differences unless both runs use the same compiler. WSL2 runs inside a VM.
    • Quality flags. noisy: CV above the threshold; cold start: first repetition much slower than the rest (no warm-up in the benchmark); few reps: too few repetitions for a reliable CI.
